IS 9003:2026
PROTECTIVE HELMETS FOR TWO-WHEELER RIDERS — REQUIREMENTS AND TEST METHODS

1. SCOPE
1.1 This standard specifies the performance requirements and test methods for protective helmets
designed and manufactured for use by riders and pillion passengers on two-wheeled motor vehicles
(motorcycles, scooters, and mopeds). This standard applies to:
    (a) Full-face helmets (covering face, jaw, and chin)
    (b) Open-face (three-quarter) helmets
    (c) Half-shell (half-face) helmets
1.2 This standard does not apply to bicycle helmets, sports helmets, industrial safety helmets,
or military helmets.
1.3 BIS certification (IS mark) under this standard is mandatory for all helmets sold in India
as per the Central Motor Vehicles Rules.

2. REFERENCES
2.1 IS 4151:2015 — Protective helmets for scooter and motor cycle riders (superseded by this standard)
2.2 IS 9003:2019 [SAMPLE] — Previous edition, superseded by this 2026 edition

3. DEFINITIONS
3.1 Shell: The outer rigid or semi-rigid component of the helmet providing the structural frame.
3.2 Impact-absorbing liner: The internal layer, typically of expanded polystyrene (EPS), that
attenuates impact energy.
3.3 Retention system: The strap, buckle, and attachment mechanism that keeps the helmet on the head.
3.4 Field of vision: The angular field within which a wearer can see while wearing the helmet.

4. CONSTRUCTION REQUIREMENTS
4.1 Shell: The shell shall be constructed from thermoplastic, thermoset, or composite material
capable of resisting penetration and distributing impact loads.
4.2 Impact-absorbing liner: The liner shall cover the entire interior surface of the shell and
be at least 15 mm thick at any point within the testing headform coverage area.
4.3 Retention system: The retention system shall include a chin strap of at least 25 mm width
and shall be equipped with a quick-release buckle operable with one hand.
4.4 Weight: The complete helmet mass shall not exceed 1.5 kg.
4.5 Field of vision: The helmet shall provide a horizontal field of vision of not less than 210°
and a vertical upward field of vision of not less than 7° from horizontal.

5. PERFORMANCE REQUIREMENTS
5.1 Impact absorption: When tested at impact points specified in Annex C, the peak linear
acceleration transmitted to the headform shall not exceed 300 g for any single impact, and the
Head Injury Criterion (HIC) shall not exceed 2 400.
5.2 Penetration resistance: A striker of 3 kg with a 60° conical tip dropped from 1 m height
shall not contact the headform.
5.3 Retention system strength: The chin strap shall not elongate more than 35 mm when subjected
to a dynamic load of 10 kg from a height of 750 mm.
5.4 Roll-off resistance: The helmet shall not detach from the headform when subjected to the
roll-off test as per Annex D.
5.5 Chin strap abrasion resistance: The chin strap shall maintain its labelling and buckle
function after 500 cycles of abrasion testing.

6. MARKING
6.1 Each helmet shall be permanently marked with:
    (a) BIS mark and licence number (mandatory)
    (b) Manufacturer name and address
    (c) Month and year of manufacture
    (d) Shell size (e.g., S, M, L, XL) and the corresponding head circumference range in mm
    (e) Shell material (abbreviated)
    (f) "Replace after any significant impact" advisory
6.2 The BIS mark shall be on both the shell exterior and the chin strap.

CLAUSE NOTES:
- Clause 5.1 (impact absorption) is the primary safety test and most commonly failed.
- Clause 4.3 (retention system) is frequently checked at point of sale.
- Helmets must carry BIS mark — purchase of non-BIS helmet is illegal under Motor Vehicle rules.
