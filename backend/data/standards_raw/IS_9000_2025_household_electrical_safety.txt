IS 9000:2025
SAFETY REQUIREMENTS FOR HOUSEHOLD ELECTRICAL APPLIANCES — GENERAL REQUIREMENTS

1. SCOPE
1.1 This standard specifies general safety requirements for household electrical appliances intended
for connection to a supply with a rated voltage not exceeding 250 V for single-phase appliances
and 480 V for other appliances. Appliances covered include, but are not limited to, cooking
appliances, heating appliances, fans, air conditioners, washing machines, refrigerators, and
similar equipment used in domestic, commercial, or light-industrial environments.
1.2 This standard applies to appliances with a rated power input not exceeding 25 kW.
1.3 This standard does not apply to appliances intended for industrial use.

2. REFERENCES
2.1 IS 1293:2019 — Plugs and socket-outlets for domestic and similar general purposes
2.2 IS 13252 (Part 1):2010 — Safety of household and similar electrical appliances (General Requirements)

3. DEFINITIONS
3.1 Rated voltage: The voltage assigned to an appliance by the manufacturer.
3.2 Rated current: The current assigned to an appliance by the manufacturer.
3.3 Protective earthing: Connection of exposed metallic parts to earth to prevent electric shock.
3.4 Insulation resistance: The ohmic resistance of the insulating material.
3.5 Dielectric strength: The ability of an insulating material to withstand high voltage without breakdown.

4. GENERAL REQUIREMENTS
4.1 Appliances shall be so constructed that, in normal use, they function safely and do not impair
the safety of persons, domestic animals, or property.
4.2 All appliances shall bear a visible mark of conformity (BIS mark) on the body, at a location
that is readable without disassembling any part of the appliance.
4.3 The rated voltage shall be marked on the appliance in numerals at least 2 mm in height.
4.4 Appliances shall comply with the creepage and clearance distances specified in Table 1 of
this standard for the applicable pollution degree and overvoltage category.
4.5 Insulating materials used in appliances shall be suitable for the temperatures they may attain
in normal operation, not degrading within the appliance's rated service life.

5. PROTECTION AGAINST ELECTRIC SHOCK
5.1 Accessible parts of an appliance shall not be live under normal conditions or under single-fault
conditions when the appliance is connected to the supply.
5.2 Appliances of Class I (earthed) shall have all conductive accessible parts connected to the
protective earth terminal. The earth continuity resistance shall not exceed 0.1 ohm when measured
with a current of at least 10 A at 50 Hz.
5.3 Appliances of Class II (double insulated) shall not require connection to a protective earth.
The supplementary insulation shall be capable of withstanding a test voltage of 3 000 V rms
for 1 minute without breakdown.
5.4 The leakage current for Class I appliances shall not exceed 0.75 mA for handheld appliances
and 3.5 mA for stationary appliances when measured at 1.1 times the rated voltage.

6. PROTECTION AGAINST OVERHEATING
6.1 Appliances shall be provided with appropriate thermal protection to prevent surface temperatures
from exceeding the limits specified in Table 2 of this standard.
6.2 Thermal protectors shall be of the self-resetting type unless otherwise specified; where
non-self-resetting protectors are used, they shall be accessible for manual reset.
6.3 Motor-operated appliances shall be provided with an overload protection device that disconnects
the motor from the supply within 5 minutes of stall.
6.4 The temperature rise on the supply cord, measured at the point of entry into the appliance,
shall not exceed 35 K above ambient (25°C reference ambient).

- Clause 4 covers marking and general construction requirements.
